Fifty-two weeks
behind the bar

A year of lesser-known classics, modern craft drinks and regional oddities for two people who have already made every Old Fashioned, Margarita and Manhattan they need to. Grouped by family so each week builds on the last. Ingredients in each recipe link straight to the buying guide, and every bottle there carries an Ohio Liquor stock check and a photo of the label. Measurements are given in both ounces and millilitres, and only two drinks call for Chartreuse — both with substitutes written into the build.

The Last Word & Its Equal-Parts Descendants

A family built on the Last Word's equal-parts template: spirit, herbal liqueur, bittersweet element, citrus. Two of these need Chartreuse, which is the one genuinely hard bottle on the list — substitutions are noted in each build. The other two need none at all.

01

Last Word

Last Word familyGin

  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) gin
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) green Chartreuse
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) maraschino liqueur (Luxardo)
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) fresh lime juice

Shake with ice; strain up into a chilled coupe; no garnish (optional cherry). If you cannot find green Chartreuse, Faccia Brutto Centerbe or Dolin Génépy le Chamois both work; the drink gets softer and less peppery but stays recognisable.

Watch · The Educated Barfly

Prohibition-era Detroit Athletic Club drink revived by Murray Stenson at Seattle's Zig Zag Café in the 2000s. The blueprint for everything else in this group.

02

Naked & Famous

Last Word familyMezcal

  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) mezcal
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) yellow Chartreuse
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) Aperol
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) fresh lime juice

Shake; double-strain up into a coupe; no garnish. Strega substitutes for yellow Chartreuse — sweeter and more saffron-forward, so pull it back to⅔ oz (20 ml) and add a touch more lime.

Watch · The Educated Barfly

Joaquín Simó, Death & Co, 2011. He described it as the bastard love child of a Last Word and a Paper Plane, conceived in the mountains of Oaxaca.

Negroni & Boulevardier Riffs

Spirit, bitter liqueur, fortified wine. Run the same structure through whiskey, gin, rum and tequila and you get four genuinely different drinks — the clearest demonstration on this list of how much the base carries.

05

Boulevardier

Negroni riffBourbon / Rye

  • 1½ oz (45 ml) bourbon or rye
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) sweet vermouth
  • ¾ oz (22.5 ml) Campari

Stir; strain over a large cube in a rocks glass; orange twist. The 1927 original is equal parts; Difford's favours 3:2:2.

Watch · The Educated Barfly

The whiskey Negroni, from Harry McElhone's Barflies and Cocktails (1927) and credited to Erskine Gwynne.

06

White Negroni

Negroni riffGin

  • 1½ oz (45 ml) gin
  • ¾–1 oz (30 ml) Suze (gentian liqueur)
  • ¾–1 oz (30 ml) Lillet Blanc

Stir; rocks or up; grapefruit or lemon twist. A lighter 1.5 : 0.75 : 0.75 keeps Suze in check.

Watch · The Educated Barfly

Wayne Collins, c. 2001. Gentian and Lillet in place of Campari and sweet vermouth — pale, dry, and bitter in an entirely different register.

  • ½ oz (15 ml) maraschino liqueur

Tiki & Rum-Forward

The most ingredient-hungry group on the list: orgeat, falernum, allspice dram, demerara syrup and a lot of crushed ice. It is also a tour of rum from Jamaica to Barbados to Martinique, ending with a drink that hands the base over to Campari.

27

  • 1½ oz (45 ml) pineapple juice
  • ½ oz (15 ml) fresh lime juice
  • ½ oz (15 ml) demerara syrup (2:1)

Shake; strain over crushed ice; pineapple wedge and cherry.

Watch · The Educated Barfly

Created 6 July 1973 at the Aviary Bar of the Kuala Lumpur Hilton by Jeffrey Ong Swee Teik. The drink that proved Campari belongs in the tropics.

Clarified Milk Punch

Modern techniqueVodka / Rum / Brandy

  • 12 oz (360 ml) spirit
  • 8 oz (240 ml) citrus juice
  • 6 oz (180 ml) sugar or syrup
  • spices or tea to taste
  • 8 oz (240 ml) hot whole milk

Stir the acidic mixture into the hot milk — never the reverse. Rest 2–24 hours refrigerated, then gravity-filter through a coffee filter until crystal clear. Serve 3–5 oz (90–150 ml) over a large cube.

Before you start

  • Every drink has a video. All 52 now link to a tutorial. The original 38 were checked against YouTube's oEmbed endpoint: every one resolves, sits on the channel claimed, and carries a title matching its drink. The remaining 14 were sourced by hand afterwards and have not been through that check. Links do rot over time; re-running the check periodically is on the backlog.
  • Two links come with asterisks. The Fish House Punch link is a YouTube Short rather than a full tutorial. The Cosmopolitan link is confirmed to be the Educated Barfly's 1934 variation, not the modern Cecchini build in the spec above — treat it as background, not as instructions.
  • Specs legitimately vary. The Boulevardier, White Negroni, Vieux Carré, Division Bell, Mai Tai and Aviation all have competing accepted ratios. The versions here are cross-checked against Difford's, Punch, Imbibe, Death & Co and Smuggler's Cove — treat them as starting points and adjust to your palate.
  • Chartreuse is now down to two drinks. The Carthusian monks capped production in 2021 and moved to strict allocation in early 2023, and that policy has not shifted since — the shortage is a deliberate decision, not a supply-chain problem, and it hits the US far harder than Europe. Rather than build a year around a bottle you may never find, only the Last Word and the Naked & Famous call for it, and both list a substitute in the build. Faccia Brutto Centerbe is the closest green stand-in; Strega covers the yellow.
  • Classification involved judgement calls. The Black Manhattan is counted as amaro-forward because Averna fully replaces the vermouth, and the Aviation is tallied in the bitter column to keep the spirit spread even. Move them if you disagree — the drinks are unaffected.
  • Metric figures are rounded to a 30 ml jigger. The convention most metric recipes use, so ¾ oz reads as 22.5 ml rather than the literal 22.2. Close enough that the balance holds; use one system throughout a given drink rather than mixing them.
Fifty-two drinks across eleven families. Sequence them by season rather than by number: stirred and spirit-forward in winter, gin and agave in spring, rum and tiki through summer, amaro in autumn.
